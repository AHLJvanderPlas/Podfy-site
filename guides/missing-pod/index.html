Why proof of delivery documents go missing

Most POD losses trace to the same root cause: the document was created at the delivery point but had to physically travel back to the office. That journey is where things break down.

The paper return loop. A driver signs the CMR at the gate, keeps a copy in the cab, and returns to depot eventually — sometimes days later, sometimes on a different vehicle, sometimes not at all if the driver changes route mid-week. The 48–120 hour gap is where the document disappears.

Sub-contracted delivery segments. If a 3PL hands a shipment to a sub-carrier for the final leg, the POD process is now outside your direct control. Sub-drivers may use different systems, lose paper, or simply not return documents through your channel.

Informal workarounds. WhatsApp photos of signed CMRs are common but unreliable — untagged, impossible to search, and easily missed in a busy chat thread. Email attachments go to inboxes that aren't monitored or searchable by reference.

  • Paper return loop: 48–120 hour delay
  • Sub-carrier handoffs break the document chain
  • WhatsApp photos — unstructured and unsearchable
  • Driver turnover: departing driver takes the documents
  • Multi-stop routes: wrong copies returned for wrong deliveries
  • No reference on the document: can't match to the invoice

Immediate recovery steps

Step 01

Contact the driver directly

If you know which driver handled the delivery, ask immediately. Many missing PODs are still in the cab — unsent, unfiled. Request a photo via WhatsApp as a stopgap while you arrange a formal copy.

Step 02

Ask the consignee

The receiving party may have their copy of the signed CMR or delivery note. A written email confirmation from the consignee stating date, time, and reference can substitute as secondary evidence in most commercial disputes.

Step 03

Check digital records

If you use a TMS, carrier portal, or any upload system, check for any digital record under the shipment reference. Even an incomplete or low-quality record is better than nothing when a customer disputes payment.

How missing PODs affect invoicing

The direct financial impact of a missing POD is an invoice put on hold. In most B2B logistics relationships, the customer's accounts payable team will not approve a freight invoice without delivery confirmation. For a carrier running €300,000/month in freight, a 5% POD gap means €15,000 in invoices are delayed at any given time.

The secondary impact is dispute escalation. A missing POD that cannot be recovered within 2 weeks typically becomes a formal dispute. Resolution involves your commercial team, the customer's procurement team, and sometimes external mediation — all for a document that cost €0 to capture digitally at the gate.

For finance teams, the metric to watch is Days Sales Outstanding (DSO). Every day a POD is missing adds to DSO for that invoice. Operations with poor POD capture rates consistently show higher DSO than the industry average.
